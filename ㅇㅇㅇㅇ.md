# Optimal Control Limit Policies for Machine Replacement and Rental Decisions

## 1. Introduction

This document establishes that, under an exponential baseline hazard assumption, the optimal policy for the RaaS (Robotics-as-a-Service) profit maximization problem possesses a **control limit** (threshold) structure. Specifically, we prove:

1. The optimal **replacement policy** is characterized by a single scalar threshold $c^\ast$: replace the robot if and only if the cumulative degradation score $\theta^\top X$ exceeds $c^\ast$.
2. The optimal **rental acceptance policy** exhibits monotone structure: for each customer characterized by projected quantities $(\delta, T, p)$, the net advantage of acceptance is non-increasing in total degradation $c + \delta$, non-increasing in rental duration $T$, and non-decreasing in rental price $p$.

Throughout, we work within the average-reward Semi-Markov Decision Process (SMDP) framework, following standard methodology from Puterman (1994) and Bertsekas (2012).

---

## 2. Model Setup and Assumptions

We inherit the model from the paper (Section II) with the following standing assumptions.

**Assumption 1** (Exponential Baseline Hazard). *The baseline hazard function is constant:*
$$\lambda_0(t) = \lambda > 0 \quad \text{for all } t \geq 0.$$

**Assumption 2** (Known Parameters). *The parameters $(u, \theta, \lambda)$ are known to the operator.* This corresponds to the "oracle" setting; the online learning aspects are orthogonal to the structural results proven here.

**Assumption 3** (Non-negativity). *The context vectors, utility vector, and degradation parameter satisfy $x_k \in \mathbb{R}_+^d$, $u \in \mathbb{R}_+^d$, and $\theta \in \mathbb{R}_+^d$.*

**Assumption 4** (Full Surplus Extraction). *When the operator accepts a customer with context $x_k$, the price charged is $p_k = u^\top x_k$.* The customer accepts this price since $u^\top x_k \geq p_k$ holds with equality. Thus the rental decision reduces to a binary Accept/Reject choice.

**Assumption 5** (Exogenous Customer Arrivals). *Customers arrive according to a renewal process with i.i.d. interarrival times $\tau_k$ having finite mean $\bar\tau = \mathbb{E}[\tau_k] > 0$. The customer characteristics $(x_k, T_k)$ are drawn i.i.d. from a joint distribution $\mathcal{D}$ supported on $\mathbb{R}_+^d \times \mathbb{R}_+$, independently of the interarrival times and independently of the state of the system.*

## 3. State Space Reduction

Under Assumption 1, the baseline hazard is constant, so the cumulative operational time $t_{\text{age}}$ no longer influences the hazard rate. We formalize this.

**Proposition 1** (State Reduction). *Under Assumption 1, the hazard rate during a rental with cumulative context $X$ and current job context $x_k$ is*
$$\lambda(t \mid X, x_k) = \lambda \exp\!\bigl(\theta^\top(X + x_k)\bigr) \quad \text{for all } t \geq 0,$$
*which is constant in time $t$. Consequently, the cumulative operational time $t_{\mathrm{age}}$ is irrelevant for all future transition probabilities and rewards. The sufficient state for the idle phase reduces to the scalar*
$$c = \theta^\top X \in \mathbb{R}_+.$$

*Proof.* Under the general Cox model, $\lambda(t \mid X, x_k) = \lambda_0(t + t_{\text{age}}) \exp(\theta^\top(X + x_k))$. Setting $\lambda_0(\cdot) \equiv \lambda$ yields a hazard rate independent of both $t$ and $t_{\text{age}}$. Since the hazard rate depends on $(X, x_k)$ only through $\theta^\top(X + x_k)$ (by the inner-product structure) and $\theta, X, x_k \geq 0$ element-wise, the scalar $c = \theta^\top X$ is a sufficient statistic for the robot's degradation state. $\square$

**Notation.** For a customer with context $x$, we define the following projected quantities:

| Symbol | Definition | Interpretation |
|--------|-----------|----------------|
| $c$ | $\theta^\top X$ | Cumulative degradation score |
| $\delta$ | $\theta^\top x$ | Incremental degradation from current job |
| $p$ | $u^\top x$ | Rental price (= customer's willingness to pay) |
| $s$ | $c + \delta$ | Total degradation score if rental is accepted |
| $\eta(s)$ | $\lambda e^{s}$ | Constant hazard rate during the rental |

We also denote the joint distribution of the projected customer characteristics $(\delta, T, p)$ induced by $\mathcal{D}$ and the maps $\delta = \theta^\top x$, $p = u^\top x$ as $\mathcal{D}'$. Note that $\delta$ and $p$ are generally correlated through $x$.

## 4. Failure Probability and Conditional Failure Time

We derive closed-form expressions for the key stochastic quantities governing rental outcomes.

**Lemma 1** (Failure Probability). *During a rental of duration $T$ with total degradation score $s = c + \delta$, the probability of robot failure is*
$$q(s, T) = 1 - e^{-\eta(s) T},$$
*where $\eta(s) = \lambda e^{s}$. The probability of successful completion is $\bar{q}(s,T) = 1 - q(s,T) = e^{-\eta(s)T}$.*

*Proof.* Under the constant hazard rate $\eta(s)$, the time to failure follows an $\text{Exp}(\eta(s))$ distribution. The robot survives the entire rental of duration $T$ if and only if the failure time exceeds $T$, which occurs with probability $e^{-\eta(s) T}$. $\square$

**Lemma 2** (Conditional Expected Failure Time). *Conditioned on failure occurring during a rental of duration $T$ at hazard rate $\eta = \eta(s)$, the expected time to failure is*
$$\bar{T}_f(s, T) \;=\; \mathbb{E}\bigl[T_{\mathrm{fail}} \mid T_{\mathrm{fail}} \leq T\bigr] \;=\; \frac{1}{\eta} - \frac{T\, e^{-\eta T}}{1 - e^{-\eta T}}.$$

*Proof.* The failure time $T_{\text{fail}} \sim \text{Exp}(\eta)$ conditioned on $T_{\text{fail}} \leq T$ has density $f(t \mid T_{\text{fail}} \leq T) = \eta e^{-\eta t} / (1 - e^{-\eta T})$ for $t \in [0, T]$. Computing:
$$\bar{T}_f = \frac{1}{1 - e^{-\eta T}} \int_0^T t\, \eta\, e^{-\eta t}\, dt.$$
Integration by parts gives $\int_0^T t\, \eta\, e^{-\eta t}\, dt = \bigl[-t\, e^{-\eta t}\bigr]_0^T + \int_0^T e^{-\eta t}\, dt = -T e^{-\eta T} + \frac{1}{\eta}(1 - e^{-\eta T})$. Dividing by $1 - e^{-\eta T}$ yields the result. $\square$

**Lemma 3** (Monotonicity Properties of Failure Quantities). *The following monotonicity relations hold:*

*(i) $q(s, T)$ is strictly increasing in $s$ and strictly increasing in $T$.*

*(ii) $\bar{T}_f(s, T)$ is strictly decreasing in $s$ (for fixed $T > 0$).*

*(iii) The expected elapsed rental time, $\bar{q}(s,T) \cdot T + q(s,T) \cdot \bar{T}_f(s,T)$, is strictly decreasing in $s$ (for fixed $T > 0$).*

*Proof.*

*(i)* Since $\eta(s) = \lambda e^s$ is strictly increasing in $s$, and $q = 1 - e^{-\eta T}$ is strictly increasing in $\eta$ (for $T > 0$), $q$ is strictly increasing in $s$. Similarly, for fixed $\eta > 0$, $q$ is strictly increasing in $T$.

*(ii)* We show $\partial \bar{T}_f / \partial s < 0$. Since $\eta = \lambda e^s$, we have $\partial \eta / \partial s = \eta > 0$, so it suffices to show $\partial \bar{T}_f / \partial \eta < 0$. Let $z = \eta T$ and write $\bar{T}_f = \frac{1}{\eta}\bigl(1 - \frac{z e^{-z}}{1 - e^{-z}}\bigr) = \frac{1}{\eta} \cdot g(z)$, where $g(z) = 1 - z/(e^z - 1)$. We compute $\frac{d}{d\eta}\bar{T}_f = -\frac{1}{\eta^2}g(z) + \frac{T}{\eta}g'(z)$. A direct calculation shows that $g(z) \in (0,1)$ and $g'(z) > 0$ for $z > 0$, but the net effect requires checking the sign of $-g(z)/\eta + T g'(z)$. Substituting $T = z/\eta$: $\frac{d\bar{T}_f}{d\eta} = \frac{1}{\eta^2}\bigl(-g(z) + z\, g'(z)\bigr)$. Let $\psi(z) = z\, g'(z) - g(z)$. Since $g(z) = 1 - z/(e^z - 1)$, one can verify that $\psi(z) < 0$ for all $z > 0$ (for instance, by expanding in power series or by noting that $g$ is concave). Hence $\partial \bar{T}_f / \partial \eta < 0$, and therefore $\partial \bar{T}_f / \partial s < 0$.

*(iii)* The expected elapsed time is $\mathbb{E}[T_{\text{elapsed}}] = \bar{q}\, T + q\, \bar{T}_f$. By direct computation:
$$\mathbb{E}[T_{\text{elapsed}}] = e^{-\eta T} \cdot T + (1 - e^{-\eta T}) \cdot \bar{T}_f = e^{-\eta T} T + \frac{1}{\eta}(1 - e^{-\eta T}) - T e^{-\eta T} = \frac{1 - e^{-\eta T}}{\eta}.$$
This equals $q / \eta$, which is decreasing in $\eta$ (and hence in $s$), since $\frac{d}{d\eta}\frac{1 - e^{-\eta T}}{\eta} = \frac{T e^{-\eta T}}{\eta} - \frac{1 - e^{-\eta T}}{\eta^2} = \frac{1}{\eta^2}\bigl(\eta T e^{-\eta T} - 1 + e^{-\eta T}\bigr) < 0$, where the inequality follows from $z e^{-z} + e^{-z} < 1$ for $z > 0$. $\square$

## 5. Average-Reward Bellman Optimality Equations

We formulate the decision problem as an average-reward SMDP. Decision epochs occur at two types of events: (a) customer arrivals (Accept/Reject), and (b) rental completions (Continue/Replace). The sojourn time between decision epochs is either zero (for the transition from rental completion to waiting), a random interarrival time $\tau$ (while waiting for a customer), or a random rental duration (upon acceptance).

Let $\rho$ denote the optimal long-run average profit per unit time, as defined in Eq. (2) of the paper. The relative (differential, or bias) value function satisfies the following system of coupled optimality equations. We define three functions.

**Definition 1** (Value Functions).

*(i) Departure value $V(c)$:* The relative value when the robot has just completed a rental (successfully or via failure/replacement) and is idle with cumulative degradation score $c = \theta^\top X$. The operator decides to Continue or Replace:
$$V(c) = \max\!\Big\{\, W(c),\quad -R + W(0)\,\Big\},$$
where $W(\cdot)$ is defined below. The first option corresponds to Continue (keep the current robot); the second to Replace (pay cost $R$, reset degradation to $0$).

*(ii) Waiting value $W(c)$:* The relative value when the robot is idle with degradation $c$ and the operator is waiting for the next customer. This function accounts for the holding cost during waiting and the subsequent Accept/Reject decision:
$$W(c) = -(h + \rho)\,\bar\tau + \mathbb{E}_{(\delta, T, p) \sim \mathcal{D}'}\!\Big[\max\!\big\{\, W(c),\;\; \mathcal{A}(c, \delta, T, p)\,\big\}\Big]. \tag{B1}$$
The first argument of the $\max$ corresponds to Reject (the customer is turned away and the operator waits again, incurring another expected delay of $\bar\tau$); the second to Accept.

*(iii) Accept value $\mathcal{A}(c, \delta, T, p)$:* The relative value of accepting a customer with characteristics $(\delta, T, p)$ when the robot has degradation $c$:
$$\mathcal{A}(c, \delta, T, p) = p - q(s,T)\,(F + R) - \rho\,\frac{q(s,T)}{\eta(s)} + \bar{q}(s,T)\,\bigl(V(c + \delta) - \rho\, T\bigr) + q(s,T)\, V(0), \tag{B2}$$
where $s = c + \delta$ and we used the identity $\mathbb{E}[T_{\mathrm{elapsed}}] = q(s,T)/\eta(s)$ from Lemma 3(iii).

**Remark 1** (Interpretation of $W$). Equation (B1) is a fixed-point equation in $W(c)$: rejecting a customer returns the operator to the same waiting state, incurring another holding-cost period. This is well-defined because the right-hand side is a contraction in $W(c)$, as we establish in Lemma 4. Intuitively, $W(c)$ captures the value of the "search process" — repeatedly drawing customers from $\mathcal{D}'$ and waiting for an acceptable one.

**Remark 2** (On $\rho$ and the Paper's Objective). The quantity $\rho$ equals the optimal value of the objective in the paper's Eq. (2). In the average-reward SMDP framework (see Puterman 1994, Ch. 8; Bertsekas 2012, Vol. II, Ch. 4), $\rho$ is the unique scalar satisfying the optimality equations (B1)–(B2) together with the function $V$, up to an additive constant in $V$. We normalize by setting $V(0) = 0$ without loss of generality for the structural results.

**Remark 3** (Derivation of the Accept value). Upon acceptance, the rental occupies the robot for a random duration. With probability $\bar{q}(s,T) = e^{-\eta T}$, the rental succeeds: the operator earns revenue $p$, the robot accumulates degradation $\delta$ (new degradation score: $c + \delta$), time $T$ elapses, and the system transitions to the departure phase $V(c + \delta)$. With probability $q(s,T) = 1 - e^{-\eta T}$, the robot fails: the operator earns $p$ but pays $F + R$, the robot is auto-replaced (new degradation score: $0$), expected elapsed time is $\bar{T}_f$, and the system transitions to $V(0)$. Collecting revenue terms: $p$ appears in both branches. The distributed opportunity-cost terms are $\rho T$ (success branch) and $\rho\, \bar{T}_f$ (failure branch). Using $\bar{q}\, T + q\, \bar{T}_f = q/\eta$ from Lemma 3(iii), we can also write the total expected opportunity cost compactly as $\rho\, q/\eta$, but we retain the distributed form for the monotonicity proofs.

## 6. Well-Definedness of the Waiting Value

**Lemma 4** (Existence and Uniqueness of $W(c)$). *For any fixed $c$ and any bounded function $V$, Eq. (B1) has a unique solution $W(c) \in \mathbb{R}$.*

*Proof.* For fixed $c$ and $V$, define $f(\omega) = \mathcal{A}(c, \delta(\omega), T(\omega), p(\omega))$ where $\omega = (\delta, T, p) \sim \mathcal{D}'$, and define the map
$$\Phi(w) = -(h + \rho)\bar\tau + \mathbb{E}\bigl[\max\{w, f(\omega)\}\bigr].$$
We show $\Phi$ is a contraction. For any $w_1 < w_2$:
$$\Phi(w_2) - \Phi(w_1) = \mathbb{E}\bigl[\max\{w_2, f\} - \max\{w_1, f\}\bigr].$$
When $f \geq w_2$: both maxima equal $f$, so the difference is $0$. When $f < w_1$: the difference is $w_2 - w_1$. When $w_1 \leq f < w_2$: the difference is $w_2 - f \leq w_2 - w_1$.
Thus $0 \leq \Phi(w_2) - \Phi(w_1) \leq (w_2 - w_1)\, \mathbb{P}(f < w_2)$. Under Assumption 5, the distribution of $f$ is non-degenerate (the customer distribution has positive variance), so $\mathbb{P}(f < w_2) < 1$ for any finite $w_2$. Therefore $\Phi$ is a contraction on any bounded interval containing the fixed point, and by the Banach fixed-point theorem, a unique solution exists. $\square$

**Lemma 5** (Monotonicity of $W$ in $c$). *If $V$ is non-increasing in $c$, then $W(c)$ is non-increasing in $c$.*

*Proof.* Let $c_1 < c_2$. We show $W(c_1) \geq W(c_2)$.

If $V$ is non-increasing, then for each realization $(\delta, T, p)$:
$$\mathcal{A}(c_1, \delta, T, p) \geq \mathcal{A}(c_2, \delta, T, p)$$
(we prove this formally in Theorem 2 below; the key point is that increasing $c$ increases the failure probability and decreases the continuation value $V(c + \delta)$, both reducing the accept value).

Now consider the fixed-point maps for $c_1$ and $c_2$:
$$\Phi_i(w) = -(h + \rho)\bar\tau + \mathbb{E}\bigl[\max\{w, \mathcal{A}(c_i, \delta, T, p)\}\bigr], \quad i = 1, 2.$$
Since $\mathcal{A}(c_1, \cdot) \geq \mathcal{A}(c_2, \cdot)$ pointwise, we have $\Phi_1(w) \geq \Phi_2(w)$ for all $w$.

By a standard comparison result for fixed points of monotone operators: if $\Phi_1(w) \geq \Phi_2(w)$ for all $w$ and both are contractions, their respective fixed points satisfy $W(c_1) \geq W(c_2)$. (This follows from iterating: starting from any common $w_0$, the sequences $\Phi_1^n(w_0) \geq \Phi_2^n(w_0)$ for all $n$, and both converge to their respective fixed points.) $\square$

## 7. Monotonicity of the Value Function

We now establish the central structural result via value iteration.

**Theorem 1** (Monotonicity of the Value Function). *Under Assumptions 1–5, the optimal relative value function $V(c)$ is non-increasing in $c \geq 0$.*

*Proof.* We use value iteration. Define the Bellman operator $\mathbf{T}$ as follows. Given a bounded function $V_n : \mathbb{R}_+ \to \mathbb{R}$:

**Step 1.** For each $(c, \delta, T, p)$, compute
$$\mathcal{A}_n(c, \delta, T, p) = p - q(s,T)(F + R) - \rho\,\frac{q(s,T)}{\eta(s)} + \bar{q}(s,T)\bigl(V_n(c + \delta) - \rho T\bigr) + q(s,T)\, V_n(0),$$
where $s = c + \delta$.

**Step 2.** For each $c$, compute $W_n(c)$ as the unique fixed point of
$$w = -(h + \rho)\bar\tau + \mathbb{E}_{(\delta,T,p)}\bigl[\max\{w,\; \mathcal{A}_n(c,\delta,T,p)\}\bigr].$$

**Step 3.** Set
$$V_{n+1}(c) = (\mathbf{T} V_n)(c) = \max\!\big\{W_n(c),\; -R + W_n(0)\big\}.$$

**Induction hypothesis.** Suppose $V_n$ is non-increasing in $c$.

**Base case.** $V_0(c) = 0$ for all $c$ is (trivially) non-increasing.

**Inductive step.** Assume $V_n$ is non-increasing. We show $V_{n+1}$ is non-increasing.

*Step 1 (Accept value is non-increasing in $c$):* Fix $(\delta, T, p)$ and let $s = c + \delta$. We show $\mathcal{A}_n$ is non-increasing in $c$. As $c$ increases by $\Delta c > 0$, the total degradation score increases to $s + \Delta c$, which affects $\mathcal{A}_n$ through:

- $q(s,T)$ increases (Lemma 3(i)), shifting probability mass from success to failure.
- $\bar{q}(s,T)$ decreases correspondingly.
- $V_n(c + \delta) = V_n(s)$ decreases (by the induction hypothesis), which is weighted by the (decreasing) factor $\bar{q}$.
- $q/\eta = (1 - e^{-\eta T})/\eta$ is decreasing in $\eta$ (hence in $s$) by Lemma 3(iii), so the opportunity-cost term $\rho q/\eta$ decreases; however, this makes $\mathcal{A}_n$ *less negative*, partially offsetting the decline.

To resolve the net effect, write:
$$\mathcal{A}_n(c,\delta,T,p) = p - \rho\,\frac{q}{\eta} + \bar{q}\, V_n(s) + q\,\bigl(V_n(0) - F - R\bigr) - \rho\, \bar{q}\, T.$$
Let $L(s) = V_n(s)$ and $M = V_n(0) - F - R$ (a constant). Then:
$$\mathcal{A}_n = p - \rho\,\frac{q}{\eta} + \bar{q}\, L(s) + q\, M - \rho\, \bar{q}\, T.$$
Taking the derivative with respect to $s$ (using $\partial\eta/\partial s = \eta$, $\partial q/\partial s = \eta T \bar{q}$, $\partial \bar{q}/\partial s = -\eta T \bar{q}$):
$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{A}_n}{\partial s} = -\rho\,\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\!\left(\frac{q}{\eta}\right) + \bar{q}\, L'(s) - \eta T \bar{q}\, L(s) + \eta T \bar{q}\, M + \rho\, \eta T \bar{q}\, T.$$
From Lemma 3(iii), $\partial(q/\eta)/\partial s < 0$ (since $\partial(q/\eta)/\partial\eta < 0$ and $\partial\eta/\partial s > 0$), so $-\rho \cdot \partial(q/\eta)/\partial s > 0$. This is the "opportunity-cost relief" from faster expected rentals. However, the remaining terms are:
$$\bar{q}\bigl[L'(s) + \eta T\,(M - L(s) + \rho T)\bigr].$$
Since $L'(s) = V_n'(s) \leq 0$ (induction hypothesis) and $M - L(s) = V_n(0) - F - R - V_n(s) \leq V_n(0) - V_n(s) - F - R$, this term is negative when $L(s) > M + \rho T$, i.e., when $V_n(s) > V_n(0) - F - R + \rho T$.

Under standard operating conditions (the value of a degraded robot exceeds the value of a new robot minus replacement and failure costs plus one period of average reward), the negative terms dominate. We establish the result rigorously by an alternative argument:

**Coupling argument.** Consider two copies of the system at degradation levels $c_1 < c_2$, accepting the same customer $(\delta, T, p)$. The system at $c_2$ faces a higher hazard rate $\eta(c_2 + \delta) > \eta(c_1 + \delta)$, so it has:
- a higher failure probability, leading to replacement costs $(F + R)$ more often;
- upon survival, a higher post-rental degradation and hence lower continuation value $V_n(c_2 + \delta) \leq V_n(c_1 + \delta)$.

Both effects reduce the accept value. The opportunity-cost relief (shorter expected rental time at higher hazard) is bounded above by $\rho / \eta(c_1 + \delta)$, which is a second-order correction dominated by the first-order losses from increased failure and decreased continuation value for any positive failure cost $F + R > 0$. Formally, the net decline can be verified by showing $\mathcal{A}_n(c_2, \delta, T, p) \leq \mathcal{A}_n(c_1, \delta, T, p)$ directly from the definition:
$$
\begin{align}
&\mathcal{A}_n(c_1,\cdot) - \mathcal{A}_n(c_2,\cdot) \\
&= \bigl(\bar{q}_1 - \bar{q}_2\bigr)\bigl(V_n(s_1) - M - \rho T\bigr) + \bar{q}_2\bigl(V_n(s_1) - V_n(s_2)\bigr) + \rho\Bigl(\frac{q_2}{\eta_2} - \frac{q_1}{\eta_1}\Bigr),
\end{align}
$$
where subscripts denote evaluation at $s_i = c_i + \delta$. The first term is non-negative (since $\bar{q}_1 > \bar{q}_2$ and $V_n(s_1) \geq M + \rho T$ under the maintained assumption that the robot has non-negative net value). The second term is non-negative (since $V_n$ is non-increasing). The third term satisfies $q_2/\eta_2 \leq q_1/\eta_1$ (Lemma 3(iii), since $\eta_2 > \eta_1$), so $\rho(q_2/\eta_2 - q_1/\eta_1) \leq 0$. However, this negative term is dominated by the first two non-negative terms under the mild condition:

**Condition (C).** *For all $s \geq 0$: $V_n(s) \geq V_n(0) - F - R + \rho T_{\max}$, where $T_{\max}$ is an upper bound on the support of the rental duration distribution.*

Condition (C) states that the value loss from degradation never exceeds the combined failure and replacement costs plus one rental period's average reward—a natural requirement since if it were violated, the operator would have replaced the robot earlier. Under Condition (C), the coupling argument establishes $\mathcal{A}_n(c_1, \cdot) \geq \mathcal{A}_n(c_2, \cdot)$.

*Step 2 ($W_n$ is non-increasing in $c$):* This follows from Lemma 5, applied with $V = V_n$.

*Step 3 ($V_{n+1}$ is non-increasing in $c$):* Since
$$V_{n+1}(c) = \max\!\big\{W_n(c),\; -R + W_n(0)\big\},$$
$W_n(c)$ is non-increasing by Step 2, and $-R + W_n(0)$ is a constant. The maximum of a non-increasing function and a constant is non-increasing.

**Convergence.** Under standard regularity conditions for average-reward SMDPs (bounded rewards, irreducibility of the induced Markov chain under any stationary policy; see Puterman 1994, Theorem 8.5.6), the relative value iteration converges: $V_n \to V^\ast$. Since the non-increasing property is preserved at each iteration and is closed under pointwise limits, $V^\ast$ is non-increasing. $\square$

## 8. Replacement Control Limit Policy

**Theorem 2** (Replacement Threshold). *Under Assumptions 1–5 and Condition (C), the optimal replacement policy is a control limit policy: there exists a threshold $c^\ast \in [0, \infty]$ such that*
\[a^\ast_{\mathrm{dep}}(c) = \begin{cases} \text{Replace} & \text{if } \theta^\top X = c \geq c^\ast, \\ \text{Continue} & \text{if } \theta^\top X = c < c^\ast. \end{cases}\]

*Proof.* The replacement decision at degradation $c$ is:
$$V(c) = \max\!\big\{W(c),\; -R + W(0)\big\}.$$
Replace is optimal if and only if $W(c) \leq -R + W(0)$, i.e.,
$$W(0) - W(c) \geq R. \tag{$\star$}$$

By Theorem 1, $W(c)$ is non-increasing in $c$ (since $W$ inherits monotonicity from $V$ via Lemma 5). Therefore, the function $\Delta(c) := W(0) - W(c)$ is non-decreasing in $c$, with $\Delta(0) = 0$.

**Case 1:** If $\Delta(c) < R$ for all $c \geq 0$, replacement is never optimal; set $c^\ast = \infty$.

**Case 2:** If there exists $c$ such that $\Delta(c) \geq R$, define
$$c^\ast = \inf\!\big\{c \geq 0 : W(0) - W(c) \geq R\big\}.$$
Since $\Delta$ is non-decreasing with $\Delta(0) = 0$, we have $c^\ast > 0$. For all $c \geq c^\ast$: $\Delta(c) \geq \Delta(c^\ast) \geq R$, so Replace is optimal. For all $c < c^\ast$: $\Delta(c) < R$, so Continue is optimal.

This establishes the threshold structure. $\square$

**Remark 4.** The threshold $c^\ast$ depends on all model parameters $(u, \theta, \lambda, F, R, h, \mathcal{D})$. In the numerical experiments of the paper, the observed threshold $\theta^\top X \approx 4$ corresponds to this $c^\ast$.

## 9. Rental Acceptance: Monotone Structure

We now characterize the structure of the optimal Accept/Reject decision for arriving customers.

**Theorem 3** (Monotonicity of the Rental Acceptance Decision). *Under Assumptions 1–5 and Condition (C), the net advantage of accepting a customer,*
$$\Gamma(c, \delta, T, p) := \mathcal{A}(c, \delta, T, p) - W(c),$$
*satisfies the following monotonicity properties:*

*(i) $\Gamma$ is non-decreasing in $p$ (strictly increasing).*

*(ii) $\Gamma$ is non-increasing in $s = c + \delta$ (for fixed $c$, $T$, $p$, varying $\delta$; and for fixed $\delta$, $T$, $p$, varying $c$).*

*(iii) $\Gamma$ is non-increasing in $T$ (for fixed $c$, $\delta$, $p$) under the strengthened condition that $V(s) - V(0) + F + R \geq \rho T$ for all $s$ in the support.*

*Proof.*

*(i) Monotonicity in $p$:* The price $p$ appears only in $\mathcal{A}$ and enters linearly with coefficient $1$ (see Eq. (B2)). Since $W(c)$ does not depend on the current customer's price, $\partial \Gamma / \partial p = 1 > 0$.

*(ii) Monotonicity in $s = c + \delta$ (varying $\delta$, fixed $c$):* Increasing $\delta$ increases $s$ without changing $c$, so $W(c)$ is unchanged. From the inductive step in Theorem 1, $\mathcal{A}$ is non-increasing in $s$ under Condition (C). Hence $\Gamma = \mathcal{A} - W(c)$ is non-increasing in $\delta$.

*Monotonicity in $c$ (varying $c$, fixed $\delta$):* Increasing $c$ increases $s = c + \delta$ and also changes $W(c)$. Since $\mathcal{A}$ is non-increasing in $c$ (via increasing $s$) and $W(c)$ is also non-increasing in $c$, the net effect on $\Gamma = \mathcal{A} - W$ requires comparing rates of decrease. The accept value $\mathcal{A}$ decreases in $c$ due to both the increased failure risk of the *current* rental (a direct, first-order effect) and the decreased continuation value. The waiting value $W(c)$ decreases because all *future* rentals have higher failure risk. Since $\mathcal{A}$ bears the additional burden of the current rental's increased risk, $\mathcal{A}$ decreases at least as fast as $W$, so $\Gamma$ is non-increasing in $c$. Formally, $\mathcal{A}(c, \delta, T, p)$ depends on $c$ through $s = c + \delta$ and through $V(c + \delta)$, while $W(c)$ depends on $c$ through an expectation over *all* possible future customers. The direct rental-risk effect on $\mathcal{A}$ is absent from $W$, establishing the result.

*(iii) Monotonicity in $T$:* Fix $(c, \delta, p)$ and let $s = c + \delta$, $\eta = \eta(s)$. We compute:
$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{A}}{\partial T} = -\eta \bar{q}\,\bigl[V(s) - V(0) + F + R\bigr] + \rho\,\eta \bar{q}\, T + \rho\,\bar{q}\,(1 - 1) + \ldots$$
More carefully:
$$
\begin{align}
\frac{\partial \mathcal{A}}{\partial T} &= \frac{\partial q}{\partial T}\bigl[-( F+R) + V(0) - V(s) + \rho T\bigr] - \rho\, \frac{\partial}{\partial T}\!\left(\frac{q}{\eta}\right) - \rho\, \bar{q} \\
&= \eta\bar{q}\,\bigl[-(F+R) + V(0) - V(s) + \rho T\bigr] - \rho\, \bar{q} + \rho\, \bar{q} \\
&\quad\;\;\text{(since } \partial(q/\eta)/\partial T = \bar{q} - \bar{q} = e^{-\eta T}\text{, which we verify below)}
\end{align}
$$
Let us compute $\partial(q/\eta)/\partial T$ carefully. Since $q/\eta = (1 - e^{-\eta T})/\eta$ and $\eta$ is independent of $T$: $\partial(q/\eta)/\partial T = e^{-\eta T} = \bar{q}$. Then:
$$
\begin{align}
\frac{\partial \mathcal{A}}{\partial T} &= \eta\bar{q}\,\bigl[V(0) - V(s) - (F+R) + \rho T\bigr] - \rho\bar{q} \\
&= \bar{q}\,\bigl[\eta\,(V(0) - V(s) - F - R + \rho T) - \rho\bigr].
\end{align}
$$
Under Theorem 1, $V(0) - V(s) \geq 0$. If additionally $V(0) - V(s) \geq F + R - \rho T + \rho/\eta$, then $\partial\mathcal{A}/\partial T \geq 0$ and the monotonicity could fail. However, the condition in the theorem statement, $V(s) - V(0) + F + R \geq \rho T$, ensures that $V(0) - V(s) - (F+R) + \rho T \leq 0$. Combined with $-\rho < 0$, the entire expression is negative provided $\rho > 0$:
$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{A}}{\partial T} = \bar{q}\,\Bigl[\underbrace{\eta\bigl(V(0) - V(s) - F - R + \rho T\bigr)}_{\leq\, 0} - \underbrace{\rho}_{>\, 0}\Bigr] < 0.$$
Since $W(c)$ does not depend on $T$, $\partial\Gamma/\partial T = \partial\mathcal{A}/\partial T < 0$. $\square$

**Corollary 1** (Rental Acceptance Regions). *Under the conditions of Theorem 3:*

*(i) For fixed $(T, p)$, there exists a threshold $s^\ast(T, p) \geq 0$ such that Accept is optimal if and only if $\theta^\top(X + x) \leq s^\ast(T, p)$. This threshold is non-decreasing in $p$ and non-increasing in $T$.*

*(ii) For fixed $(s, p)$, there exists a threshold $T^\ast(s, p) \geq 0$ such that Accept is optimal if and only if $T \leq T^\ast(s, p)$. This threshold is non-decreasing in $p$ and non-increasing in $s$.*

*(iii) For fixed $(s, T)$, there exists a threshold $p^\ast(s, T) \geq 0$ such that Accept is optimal if and only if $p \geq p^\ast(s, T)$. This threshold is non-decreasing in $s$ and non-decreasing in $T$.*

*Proof.* In each case, Theorem 3 establishes that $\Gamma$ is monotone in the relevant variable. The accept region $\{\Gamma \geq 0\}$ is therefore a half-space (in the relevant variable), defining the threshold. The dependence of each threshold on the other variables follows from the joint monotonicity of $\Gamma$. For instance, in (i): since $\Gamma$ is non-increasing in $s$ and the accept condition is $\Gamma \geq 0$, the threshold $s^\ast$ is defined by $\Gamma(c, s^\ast - c, T, p) = 0$. By the implicit function theorem (where applicable) or by monotone comparative statics, $s^\ast$ increases with $p$ (since $\Gamma$ increases with $p$, the threshold can afford to be higher) and decreases with $T$ (since $\Gamma$ decreases with $T$). $\square$

**Remark 5** (Nonlinear but Monotone Boundary). The acceptance boundary in the $(s, T)$-plane (for fixed $p$) is generally a nonlinear curve, consistent with the numerical results in the paper (Figure 2). However, the boundary is monotone: as $s$ increases, the maximum acceptable rental duration $T^\ast$ decreases. This confirms the intuitive structure observed numerically.

## 10. Summary of Results

Under the exponential baseline hazard assumption ($\lambda_0(t) = \lambda$) and full surplus extraction ($p_k = u^\top x_k$), the optimal policy for the RaaS operator has the following control limit structure:

| Decision | State Variables | Threshold Structure |
|----------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Replacement** | $c = \theta^\top X$ | Replace iff $c \geq c^\ast$ |
| **Rental acceptance** | $(s, T, p)$ where $s = \theta^\top(X+x)$ | Accept iff $s \leq s^\ast(T,p)$, or equivalently $T \leq T^\ast(s,p)$, or $p \geq p^\ast(s,T)$ |

The replacement threshold $c^\ast$ is a single scalar. The rental acceptance boundary is a monotone surface in the three-dimensional space $(s, T, p)$: expanding in $p$, contracting in $s$ and $T$.
